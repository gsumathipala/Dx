## Scheduling a round

**Phlebotomy** lists scheduled collections. Create one with the patient, ward,
scheduled time, collection type and the tests required.

| Collection type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Routine** | Ordinary ward or clinic round |
| **STAT** | Immediate |
| **Timed** | Must be taken at a specified time — drug levels, cortisol, glucose tolerance |

**Timed** collections deserve care. A trough drug level taken an hour early is
not a trough, and dosing decisions made on it can harm the patient. If a timed
collection slips, record the actual time.

## At the bedside

1. **Identify the patient actively.** Ask them to state their name and date of
   birth. Do not read it to them for confirmation — an unwell or hard-of-hearing
   patient will agree to the wrong name.
2. **Check the request** against what they tell you.
3. **Collect** in the correct tubes.
4. **Label at the bedside, in their presence**, before you leave.

> Steps 1 and 4 prevent the two commonest serious pre-analytical errors. Both
> are skipped under time pressure, and both are why laboratories reject
> unlabelled tubes rather than guessing.

## Order of draw

When filling several tubes from one venepuncture, the sequence matters:
additive carried from one tube into the next changes results.

| Order | Tube | Additive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Blood culture | — |
| 2 | Citrate (coagulation) | Sodium citrate |
| 3 | Serum / gel | Clot activator |
| 4 | Heparin | Lithium/sodium heparin |
| 5 | EDTA (haematology) | K₂/K₃ EDTA |
| 6 | Fluoride oxalate (glucose) | Sodium fluoride |

The classic failure is EDTA carried into a later tube: EDTA chelates calcium,
so the calcium reads implausibly low and potassium implausibly high — EDTA
salts are potassium salts.

## Technique that changes results

| Practice | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Prolonged tourniquet (>1 min) | Haemoconcentration: protein, calcium, potassium all rise |
| Fist clenching | Potassium rises — muscle releases it |
| Drawing above a drip | Dilution, or the infusion itself measured |
| Under-filling a citrate tube | Wrong anticoagulant ratio; coagulation results invalid |
| Vigorous mixing | Haemolysis |
| Small needle, strong vacuum | Haemolysis |

These are not minor. A tourniquet left on while the phlebotomist finds another
tube can raise potassium by several tenths of a mmol/L — enough to turn a
normal result into a flagged one.

## Recording the collection

Mark the round **Completed** with the actual collection time. That time starts
the turnaround clock and matters for timed tests and for analytes that change
in the tube.

## If you cannot collect

Record it rather than leaving the round open: a failed attempt, a patient who
refused, a patient who was absent. The ward needs to know it did not happen.
