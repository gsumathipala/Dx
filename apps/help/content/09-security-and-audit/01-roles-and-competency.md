## Two different gates

**Role** is what the system permits you to attempt.
**Competency** is whether you are assessed as able to do it.

They are independent and both apply. A biomedical scientist's role permits
result validation; competency decides *which tests* they may validate. A newly
qualified scientist has the role from day one and competency for nothing.

## What competency assessment involves

> **CLIA 42 CFR §493.1451(b)(8)** requires competency to be assessed
> **semi-annually during the first year** a person performs a test, and
> **annually thereafter**.

CLIA names six elements, and an assessment covering only one is not an
assessment:

1. Direct observation of routine performance, including preparation and
   processing.
2. Monitoring of recording and reporting of results.
3. Review of intermediate results, worksheets, QC records, proficiency results
   and maintenance records.
4. Direct observation of instrument maintenance and function checks.
5. Assessment of performance on blind specimens — previously analysed
   specimens, internal blind samples, or external proficiency material.
6. Assessment of problem-solving skills.

Elements 5 and 6 are the ones most often omitted, and they are the ones that
distinguish competence from familiarity.

## Recording it

**Settings → User competency**: the person, the test *or* a discipline
category, the assessment date, the expiry date, who assessed it, and notes.

A record scoped to a **test** covers that test. A record scoped to a
**category** covers every test in that department — useful for a scientist
competent across a whole discipline.

## Expiry is automatic

A competency record stops satisfying the gate **on its expiry date**, not when
somebody remembers to update it.

This matters. A system where competency lapses silently is one where an
inspection finds staff reporting results under expired assessments — a common
and entirely avoidable finding.

Records expiring within 30 days appear on the compliance dashboard.

## What happens when it lapses

Validation and verification are refused, naming the test. Result **entry** is
not blocked — the person can still record values; they cannot authorise them.

That split is deliberate: work continues, authorisation waits for someone
qualified.

## Who may assess

Someone qualified in the test, normally the technical supervisor or laboratory
director. CLIA specifies who may hold those positions by qualification and
experience.

The assessor is recorded, because an assessment is a professional judgement
attributable to a person.

## Training versus competency

**Training records** show what someone was taught. **Competency** records that
someone was assessed as able.

Training precedes competency and does not substitute for it. Attending a course
demonstrates attendance.
