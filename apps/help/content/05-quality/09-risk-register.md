## Why keep one

A nonconformance system is reactive: it records what already went wrong. A risk
register is proactive: what *could* go wrong, and are we ready.

> **ISO 15189:2022 §8.5** requires the laboratory to consider risks to patients
> arising from its activities, and to act on them proportionately.

## Scoring

Each risk carries a **likelihood** and a **severity**, each 1 to 5. The score is
their product.

| Score | Rating | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15–25 | **High** | Act now; escalate |
| 8–14 | **Medium** | Plan mitigation |
| 1–7 | **Low** | Accept and monitor |

Record **residual** likelihood and severity after mitigation. The residual score
is what actually governs — a high inherent risk with effective controls may be
perfectly acceptable, and saying so explicitly is the point.

## Severity means harm to the patient

Rate severity by consequence for the patient, not inconvenience to the
laboratory. A delayed routine result is an irritation; a wrong potassium can
stop a heart.

That reframing changes priorities. An analyser breakdown that halts work for a
day scores lower than a rare mislabelling route that reaches one patient, if
the mislabelling could kill them.

## Worth having on the register

| Area | Typical risks |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-analytical | Misidentification, wrong tube, delayed transport, unsuitable specimen |
| Analytical | Instrument failure, reagent supply, calibration drift, interference |
| Post-analytical | Wrong report recipient, missed critical value, transcription error |
| Information | System outage, data loss, unauthorised access, backup failure |
| People | Single points of expertise, competency lapse, staffing gaps |
| Facilities | Power, temperature, water, fire, flood |

**Single points of expertise** is the one most often missed. If one person is
the only one who can run an assay, their absence is a service outage, and their
departure is a crisis.

## Reviewing

Set a next-review date. A register written once and never revisited documents
the risks of a laboratory that no longer exists.

Review after any significant change: new method, new instrument, new premises,
a change in staffing — and after any nonconformance, which is evidence that a
risk was either unidentified or underestimated.
