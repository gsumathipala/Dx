## The question quality management answers

Not "did the analyser give a number?" but **"is this number right, and how do
we know?"**

A laboratory cannot inspect its own output the way a factory can — there is no
way to look at a potassium result and see whether it is correct. Confidence has
to be built from evidence about the *process* instead.

## The parts, and what each proves

| Part | Answers | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Quality control](/help/quality/running-quality-control/) | Is the method performing right now? | Every run |
| [Equipment records](/help/quality/equipment-and-calibration/) | Is the instrument maintained and calibrated? | Continuous |
| [Competency](/help/security-and-audit/roles-and-competency/) | Is the person qualified to do this? | 6-monthly, then annually |
| [Proficiency testing](/help/quality/proficiency-testing/) | Do we agree with other laboratories? | Several times a year |
| [Method validation](/help/quality/method-validation/) | Does the method perform as claimed *here*? | Before use, and after change |
| [Nonconformances](/help/quality/corrective-actions/) | What went wrong, and did we fix the cause? | As they arise |
| [Risk register](/help/quality/risk-register/) | What could go wrong, and are we ready? | Reviewed periodically |
| [Document control](/help/reporting/controlled-documents/) | Is everyone following the current procedure? | Continuous |

Each answers a different question. QC tells you the method worked this morning;
it tells you nothing about whether the method was fit for purpose in the first
place — that is validation. Validation tells you nothing about whether the
person running it was trained — that is competency.

Gaps appear when a laboratory has some of these and assumes they cover the rest.

## Internal versus external

**Internal quality control** — your own control material, every run. Detects
*precision* problems and drift. It cannot detect a systematic bias, because the
target values came from your own method.

**External quality assessment** (proficiency testing) — specimens from an
outside provider, results compared with other laboratories. Detects **bias**:
you are precisely wrong, consistently, and only comparison with others reveals
it.

Both are required. A laboratory with excellent internal QC and no external
assessment can be reliably, invisibly wrong for years.

## The compliance dashboard

**Compliance** gathers everything currently outstanding: open nonconformances,
overdue proficiency surveys, expired competency, calibration overdue, documents
past review, critical values unacknowledged, notifications past their deadline,
and the state of the audit trail.

It also shows which enforced controls are **on**, because a control quietly
switched off is worse than one that was never claimed.

## Standards, in one paragraph each

**CLIA** (US federal law) sets minimum requirements for any laboratory testing
human specimens: personnel qualifications, QC, proficiency testing, method
validation and records. It is law, not accreditation — non-compliance can close
a laboratory.

**CAP** accreditation goes further, with detailed checklists and peer
inspection every two years. CAP accreditation confers CLIA deemed status.

**ISO 15189** is the international standard for medical laboratories, covering
both quality management and technical competence. It is structured around
processes and risk rather than prescriptive rules.

**21 CFR Part 11** governs electronic records and signatures where FDA-regulated
records are kept electronically: audit trails, signature controls, system
validation.

These overlap heavily. A laboratory meeting ISO 15189 meets most of CLIA; the
differences are in emphasis and evidence, not intent.
