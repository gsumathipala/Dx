## Chain of custody

A chain of custody is an unbroken record of who held a specimen, when, and what
they did with it. Every transfer is recorded: collected, received, transferred,
aliquoted, stored, retrieved, shipped, disposed.

**Tracking** searches custody events by specimen or accession number and shows
the timeline.

### Why it matters

For routine clinical work it answers practical questions: where is the sample,
can it be repeated, who had it last.

For anything that may be **disputed** — a result questioned in a complaint, an
employment or forensic context, a research sample — the chain is the evidence
the specimen analysed was the specimen collected. A gap in it undermines every
result from that specimen, however good the analysis was.

## Storage

**Storage locations** form a hierarchy: freezer → rack → box → position. Each
records temperature and capacity.

Placing a specimen records where it went, who put it there and when. A position
can hold one specimen at a time — the system will not let two specimens occupy
the same place, which is how "it should be in B4" becomes "it is in B4".

### Temperature

| Temperature | Typical use |
| --- | --- |
| Room temperature | Short-term, some chemistry |
| 2–8 °C | Most serum, short to medium term |
| −20 °C | Medium-term storage |
| −70 °C or below | Long-term, nucleic acids, some proteins |

Repeated freeze–thaw cycles degrade many analytes. This is why specimens are
aliquoted before freezing rather than thawed and refrozen.

## Aliquots

An aliquot is a child specimen taken from a parent — for a referral test, for
storage, or to avoid freeze–thaw.

The aliquot keeps its link to the parent, so its own custody chain is traceable
back to the original collection.

## Retention and disposal

**Settings → Sample retention** sets how long each specimen type is kept, at
what temperature, and how it is disposed of.

Retention balances two costs: storage space against the ability to repeat or add
a test. Typical periods run from a few days for whole blood to years for serum
in some settings.

This is distinct from **record** retention, which is much longer — see
[record retention](/help/administration/record-retention/). The specimen may be
long gone while the result must be kept for years.

Disposal is recorded: which specimen, under which policy, by whom, in which
batch. Clinical waste disposal is separately regulated in most jurisdictions.
