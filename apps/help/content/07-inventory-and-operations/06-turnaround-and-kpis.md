## What turnaround time measures

Turnaround is measured from order to completion. **Turnaround** shows every open
order against its target, with warning and breach thresholds.

## Which turnaround you measure matters

There are several, and they answer different questions:

| Interval | Measures |
| --- | --- |
| Collection → result | What the **patient** experiences |
| Receipt → result | What the **laboratory** controls |
| Analysis → result | The **analytical** step alone |

Laboratories naturally report receipt-to-result, because that is what they
control. Clinicians experience collection-to-result, and a laboratory reporting
excellent TAT while clinicians complain is usually measuring a different
interval — often one that excludes a two-hour transport delay.

Measure what you control **and** what the patient experiences. The gap between
them is usually where the improvement is.

## Setting thresholds

**Settings → Turnaround thresholds** sets target, warning and breach hours, by
test, by department or globally, and by priority.

The thresholds must read **warning ≤ target ≤ breach**, so a warning is raised
before the target is missed rather than after.

Targets should come from clinical need, not current performance. Ask what delay
changes a decision: a potassium that takes four hours is useless in an emergency
department and fine in a routine clinic.

## Use the median and a percentile, not the mean

The mean is a poor summary of turnaround because the distribution has a long
tail: most work completes quickly, a few outliers take far longer, and the mean
sits somewhere describing nothing.

**KPI** reports the median and the 90th percentile.

- The **median** is typical performance.
- The **90th percentile** is what the unlucky tenth experience.

A laboratory with a median of 45 minutes and a 90th percentile of 6 hours has a
real problem that a mean of 90 minutes would hide entirely. The outliers are
also where complaints come from — nobody complains about a typical result.

## The other indicators

| Indicator | Watch for |
| --- | --- |
| Orders received and completed | Divergence means a growing backlog |
| TAT breaches | Where, and which tests |
| QC pass rate | A falling rate precedes a failure |
| Results entered | Workload |
| Completed by department | Where the load actually is |

## Using them honestly

Indicators are for finding problems, not for demonstrating their absence.

Two failure modes are worth naming. **Measuring what is easy** — counting
samples rather than the interval clinicians care about. And **managing the
metric** — reclassifying work to move it out of a breach category. Both produce
better numbers and no better service.

The useful question is always: *which patients waited too long, and why?*
