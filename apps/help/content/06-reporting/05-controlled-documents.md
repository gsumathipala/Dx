## What document control is for

A standard operating procedure is only useful if everyone is following the
**same** version, and the current one is correct.

> **ISO 15189:2022 §8.3** requires documents to be approved before use,
> reviewed periodically, identified by version, and available where they are
> used — with obsolete versions withdrawn.

The failure mode is specific and common: two versions of a procedure in
circulation, staff following whichever is pinned near their bench, and nobody
aware they differ.

## The record

**Documents** lists controlled documents with number, title, category, version,
status, effective date and review date.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Being written; not for use |
| **In review** | Under review |
| **Active** | The current version — this is what you follow |
| **Superseded** | Replaced by a newer version |
| **Obsolete** | Withdrawn entirely |

A document cannot be made **Active** without an effective date and a scheduled
review date. Both are required by the standard, and a document with no review
date is one that silently goes stale.

## Review dates

Documents past review are flagged on the compliance dashboard.

A typical cycle is two years, sooner for anything that changes often. Review
does not always mean rewriting — confirming a procedure is still correct and
recording that you did is a valid outcome.

## Acknowledgement

Where a document requires it, staff acknowledge they have read the current
version, and the acknowledgement records the **version**.

That detail matters. Acknowledging version 3 says nothing about version 4. When
a procedure changes materially, acknowledgement is required again — which is
exactly the point: it is evidence people know about the change.

Outstanding acknowledgements are visible per document.

## Writing a usable SOP

- **Write for the person doing the work**, not for an inspector.
- **Be specific.** "Centrifuge appropriately" is not a procedure; "centrifuge at
  2000 g for 10 minutes within 2 hours of collection" is.
- **Say what to do when it goes wrong**, not only when it goes right.
- **Keep it current.** A procedure everyone knows is out of date teaches staff
  that documents are decoration.
